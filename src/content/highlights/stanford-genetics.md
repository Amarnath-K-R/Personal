Between July 30 and September 1, 2025, I spent two months at Stanford University as an Undergraduate Visiting Researcher and Research Intern in the Department of Genetics, while I was in my 3rd year of Bachelor's in Computer Science, Amrita University. It was my first time living outside India for an extended period, stepping away from everything familiar and learning to find my footing in a completely new world. Looking back, I never expected those two months to leave such a lasting mark on me.

On the research side, I explored how artificial intelligence could be applied to genomics and medical research. I learned to use tools such as DIA-NN to analyse mass spectrometry data and search for proteins in flies, and developed a cross-modal framework for cell type label transfer by connecting single-cell RNA sequencing and proteomics data. It opened my eyes to the possibilities at the intersection of AI, biology, and medicine, and to how computational research can contribute to our understanding of life.

But the most profound changes happened beyond the research itself.

I still remember the feeling of holding my Stanford ID badge for the first time. Seeing my profile appear on Stanford Profiles was another moment I will never forget. There was something deeply personal about seeing my name in that place. It made everything feel real. For a moment, I could pause and appreciate how far I had come, and feel genuinely proud of myself.

But perhaps the greatest thing I brought back from Stanford wasn't a technical skill or a line on my profile. It was a change in how I saw myself.

I returned believing that the things I dream of achieving are not as distant or impossible as they once seemed. That with discipline, consistency, and the willingness to step beyond what feels familiar, I can build a future I once only imagined. I went to Stanford hoping to learn and contribute. I came back with a stronger belief in my own potential.

Two months may seem like a short time, but sometimes a short chapter can change the direction in which you see your entire future.

I will always be grateful to Stanford University and the Department of Genetics for this opportunity, and to everyone who welcomed me, guided me, and made this experience so meaningful. The research I explored and the lessons I learned have stayed with me long after those two months have passed.

Some places leave you with memories. Others leave you with a different version of yourself. Stanford will always be both for me.
